The complete rain jacket guide

Fabrics, layers, ratings and fit: everything you need to understand before choosing a jacket for wet weather.

How they work

Membranes, coatings and finishes

Most waterproof jackets rely on one of two technologies. Membranes are thin films with microscopic pores or a solid hydrophilic structure that blocks liquid water but lets vapour pass. Coatings are layers applied directly to the inside of the fabric; they are often lighter and more affordable, though usually less breathable.

On the outside, a durable water repellent (DWR) finish makes rain bead and roll off. It does not make the jacket waterproof on its own, but it keeps the face fabric from soaking up water, which protects breathability.

Ratings

Waterproof and breathability numbers

Waterproofing is measured as hydrostatic head in millimetres. Many brands consider 10,000 mm and above suitable for regular rain, and 20,000 mm and above for severe weather.

Breathability is commonly expressed as the moisture vapour transmission rate (MVTR) in g/m²/24h, or as RET, where a lower number means more breathable. Testing methods differ between brands, so compare numbers with caution.

  • Look for fully taped seams, not just critically taped seams, for all-day rain.
  • Check that the hood and zips match the fabric’s performance.

Length

Waist-length jackets suit active pursuits. Hip and thigh lengths cover more of you on walks and commutes.

Hood

A good hood turns with your head, has a peak to keep rain off your face and adjusts at the back and sides.

Pockets

Hand pockets placed higher than usual stay usable with a backpack hip belt or harness.

Fit

Getting the fit right

Try a rain jacket on over the layers you will wear underneath it. You should be able to raise your arms overhead without the hem lifting far above your waist or the sleeves exposing your wrists.

  • Reach forward as if holding handlebars and check the back does not ride up.
  • Zip the jacket fully and turn your head; the collar should not rub your chin.
  • Adjust the hood and check you can still see sideways.
  • Leave room for a fleece or light insulated layer in colder months.
Style

Rainwear that looks good

Rain jackets no longer need to look purely technical. Trenches, parkas and minimalist shells in muted tones work well with tailoring and knitwear, while bright colours add cheer and visibility on grey days.

For a polished look, match the jacket length to your outfit: a mid-thigh coat covers a blazer, while a cropped shell works best with casual wear.

Layering

Rain jackets in a layering system

Think of your rain jacket as the outer shell of a three-part system. A moisture-wicking base layer moves sweat away from your skin, a mid-layer such as fleece adds warmth, and the shell keeps wind and rain out.

Because the shell does not need to be warm on its own, you can wear the same jacket across many seasons simply by changing what goes underneath.
