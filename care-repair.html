Caring for and repairing your rain jacket

Washing, reproofing, drying, storing and simple repairs that keep your jacket waterproof, breathable and comfortable for many seasons.

Washing

How to wash a rain jacket

Washing removes the dirt, body oils and sunscreen that clog breathable fabrics and weaken water repellency. Always check the care label first.

  1. Close all zips and Velcro tabs and loosen the drawcords.
  2. Clean the detergent drawer so no residue of ordinary detergent remains.
  3. Use a technical wash or a small amount of mild, non-biological liquid detergent.
  4. Wash on a gentle cycle at the temperature on the label, often 30°C.
  5. Add an extra rinse to remove all soap.

Avoid fabric softener, bleach and powder detergents, which can damage membranes and coatings.

Reproofing

Bringing back the beading

If water no longer beads on the outer fabric after washing, the DWR needs a boost. Many finishes can be reactivated with gentle heat, such as a low tumble dry or a cool iron through a towel, if the care label allows.

When heat no longer helps, apply a new water repellent treatment. Spray-on products let you target high-wear areas like shoulders and cuffs; wash-in products treat the whole garment evenly.

For waxed cotton, warm the wax, work it into the fabric with a cloth, focusing on seams and creases, then leave the jacket somewhere warm to cure.

Drying

Hang on a wide hanger away from direct heat. Do not dry on radiators, which can damage coatings and seam tape.

Storing

Store clean and fully dry, hanging loosely in a cool place. Long-term compression in a stuff sack can crease membranes.

Travelling

Roll rather than fold tightly, and air the jacket out as soon as you arrive to prevent mildew.

Repair

Simple fixes at home

  • Small tears and punctures: clean the area and apply a waterproof repair patch or tape on the outside.
  • Peeling seam tape: re-seal with seam sealer suited to the fabric, or ask a repair service to re-tape the seam.
  • Sticky zips: clean grit with a soft brush and use a zip lubricant designed for outdoor gear.
  • Lost cord toggles: replacement toggles thread on easily and restore a snug fit.

For bigger damage, specialist outdoor repair services can often fix jackets for far less than a replacement.

Lifespan

Signs your jacket needs attention

  • Damp patches inside the same areas after every walk.
  • The outer fabric darkens and soaks up water quickly.
  • Seam tape is lifting at the shoulders or hood.
  • The inner layer feels flaky or sticky.

Often a thorough wash and reproof solves the first two. The last sign usually means the coating or laminate has reached the end of its life.
